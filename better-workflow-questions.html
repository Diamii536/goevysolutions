Process improvement

Better workflows begin with better questions

Before you redesign a process, understand what it is meant to achieve.

GOEVY PERSPECTIVES

4 min read

← All insights

Start with the outcome

A process is a way of delivering an outcome. Ask who needs that outcome, what good looks like, and what information or action is required to achieve it. This keeps improvement focused on useful work.

Ask the people doing the work

Invite the team to walk through a real example. Where did it wait? Which information was missing? What had to be repeated? What happened outside the documented process? These questions reveal the difference between the process on paper and the work in practice.

Question each handover

For every stage, ask: who owns the next action, what tells them to start, and how do they know it is complete? Clarifying those three points can remove uncertainty without introducing a new tool.

Test the change in a small setting

Try the redesigned flow with a limited team or a manageable type of request. Agree what you will measure, gather feedback, and adjust before expanding. Record the decisions so the next person can follow the process without relying on memory.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Choose one process, one clear owner, and one useful measure. Start there, then learn from what changes.
