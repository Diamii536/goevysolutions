Data & decisions

Measure what helps you make a better decision

A useful KPI tells someone when to act, and what to look at next.

GOEVY PERSPECTIVES

4 min read

← All insights

Begin with a decision

Before adding another chart, identify a decision the business needs to make. Should work be reassigned? Is a delivery commitment at risk? Which recurring issue needs attention? The decision gives a measure its purpose.

Define the measure clearly

Specify what is counted, where the data comes from, how often it is updated, and who owns it. A shared definition matters as much as the number. Two teams using different definitions can reach different conclusions from apparently similar reports.

Use a balanced view

Speed alone can hide rework. Volume alone can hide declining service. Pair a measure of activity or delivery with a measure of quality and a view of the customer outcome. Keep the set small enough that the team can review it regularly.

Connect reporting to action

Give each review a practical purpose: understand a change, agree an action, assign an owner, and decide when to check again. Reporting becomes useful when it helps the team change what happens next.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Choose one process, one clear owner, and one useful measure. Start there, then learn from what changes.
