Business systems

When growth exposes the gaps in the way you work

Growth adds complexity. A better operating system makes that complexity manageable.

GOEVY PERSPECTIVES

5 min read

← All insights

Growth changes the work

A growing business adds people, customers, tools, and decisions. The informal arrangements that once kept everything moving can become harder to follow. Work waits for one person, teams repeat the same checks, and managers spend more time finding information than acting on it.

Look at the handovers

Start with a single journey: an enquiry becoming an order, a project reaching delivery, or a request moving to approval. Write down each handover, the information it needs, and the person who owns the next action. The places where ownership or information becomes unclear are useful starting points for improvement.

Fix one important flow first

Choose a process that matters to customers and is small enough to improve. Agree on the current problem, record a baseline, and involve the people doing the work. A useful first change might be a clearer intake form, a shared definition of completion, or an explicit decision owner.

Make improvement part of the routine

Review what changed with the team. Did the work become easier to complete? Did waiting time fall? Are exceptions visible? Keep what helps, adjust what does not, and document the new way of working before moving to the next priority.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Choose one process, one clear owner, and one useful measure. Start there, then learn from what changes.
